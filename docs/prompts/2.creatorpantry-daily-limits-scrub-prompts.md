# CreatorPantry — Plan Daily Limits SCRUB Prompts

Atomic prompts that set, enforce and validate each plan's daily picture limit, charge pictures against the
credit allowance, and make the plan's credits the account's real allowance — so that what the landing page's
pricing, credits and promises sections say is what the product does. For use with Claude Code and the
CreatorPantry `.claude/` toolkit. Path in the repo: `docs/prompts/2.creatorpantry-daily-limits-scrub-prompts.md`.

This document is a narrow slice of `docs/prompts/1.creatorpantry-ai-pricing-scrub-prompts.md` (AP.*). It
delivers the parts of AP.2.4, AP.2.5, AP.4.1, AP.4.2 and AP.4.4 that the landing page already claims, in a
form that needs **no quality classes and no Stripe**. It does not redefine anything in `CLAUDE.md`,
`.claude/rules/` or the AP decisions; where it narrows an AP prompt, the relationship table below says how.

## Decisions already made

1. **Billing scope.** Plans and metering are in scope on the owner's request (AP decision 1). Payment is not:
   nothing here takes money. A plan is set by configuration or by ops until Phase 6 of the AP document.
2. **A plan is per Identity account, across workspaces** (AP decision 4, USAGE-001). The daily picture cap is
   therefore the account's, and two workspaces the same person works in share it.
3. **The per-workspace cap stays.** `ImageVariantsPerWorkspacePerDay` remains a runaway bound on every
   workspace, beside the plan's. A request must pass both.
4. **One kind of picture.** There are no quality classes (AP.3.1 is not built, and the landing page no longer
   names any). One picture rate applies to every picture until AP.3.1 keys rates by class.
5. **The day is UTC**, as `LimitDay` already defines it, and a refusal says so and says when it resets.
6. **Prices are still proposals.** `Billing:PublishPlans` stays `false` outside Development. Enforcing the
   caps makes the page's statements about them true; it does not decide the numbers. AP.1.1 does.
7. **Limits in code, never in a model** (ai.md): no model sees a balance, a cap or a plan.

## Open, for the owner in DL.1

- **The default plan.** What an account with no plan row is on. Proposed: `trial`, read from a new
  `Billing:DefaultPlan`, so a new sign-up gets the Trial's 5 pictures a day. Until AP.7.1, the trial does not
  expire.
- **What counts against the day.** Proposed: every variant **requested** is held at admission. A variant the
  provider generated or flagged is spent (Venice bills a flagged one). A variant never sent, because the
  provider was unavailable, the request was cancelled first or it failed before generation, is released and
  can be requested again the same day.
- **Charge pictures before Stripe exists.** Proposed: yes. Closing the image cost leak needs no billing (AP
  decision 2), and the landing page already says pictures cost credits.
- **Which credit-allowance gate refuses first.** Proposed order at the request: daily plan cap, then the
  per-workspace cap, then credits. The cap tells the creator when to come back; credits do not reset today.

**Fixed (owner approved the four blockers plus two smaller items, 2026-10-30):**

1. ***A lease lost at a bill write no longer carries the pass on.*** `BillAsync` returns false and sets
   `LeaseLost` when another worker has taken the operation, and all nine call sites in the inspection and
   staging switches discarded that answer and returned "go on". The loop then bought the next variant from the

   `Unavailable` site is untouched — it recorded `Retryable` and stopped already.
2. ***The platform's day gets its micros back when the sweep expires a hold.*** `spend.ReturnAsync` had exactly
   one caller, `ReleaseImageAsync`, and the sweep calls `ExpireAsync` — so every abandoned hold kept its list
   price on `PlatformImageSpendDay` for the rest of that UTC day. The breaker then trips for **every** account
   on pictures nobody can still make. The give-back now hangs off `ExpireAsync`, so each of its callers gets it
   once, and `ReleaseImageAsync` delegates. A **settled** hold still keeps its whole charge, which is the
   documented decision ("the counter can read high and never low") and was never the defect.
3. ***`CheckImageHoldAsync` reads the clock, not just the status.*** It matched `Held` with no `ReleasedAt` and
   never compared `ExpiresAt`, so for up to one sweep interval a hold past its expiry read `Live`: the worker
   generated against it and settlement, finding it expired, charged nothing — pictures made, nobody billed, and
   the day's count handed back under them.
4. ***The quota failure summary names no account state.*** "AI access was switched off for this account…" and
   "This account's free trial ended…" were stored on the operation, and `GetOperationAsync` has no role gate
   because the routes it feeds are Viewer too — so a colleague reading that operation learnt when a creator's
   trial ran out. Both now read "AI work was not available when these pictures were to be made." The creator
   learns which it was from their own usage and billing pages. This is the audit's "refusals disclose nothing
   about anyone else", and it was the only disclosure found.
5. ***The worker writes nothing against a workspace it could not resolve.*** When `ResolveAsync` failed,
   `SettleAbandonedAsync` fell through both catches to `AbandonUnsettledAsync` and ended the operation anyway.
   Finding the row is not authorization (`tenancy.md`'s queue-claim carve-out), so it is now left alone and said
   out loud. The trade is that a workspace which never resolves leaves its operations Running to be re-swept;
   that is an operator-visible warning rather than an unvalidated write.
6. ***An admitted hold is always reported to its caller.*** `PackBalanceAsync` and `NoticeLowCreditAsync` run
   after the hold commits, and the caller learns the reservation id — the only thing that can release the hold —
   from the return. A throw there stranded the hold for the full `GenerationHoldDuration`. Both are now advisory,
   cancellation included, because a caller that gave up still has to be told what to release.

   on resume, and `SettledAmount` was `PerAttemptEstimate × BilledVariants` with no cap while the day's count was

**Not defects, though they were reported as the top finding of their review:**
- *A replay spends a rate-limit token.* `OperationLimitFilter` is a resource filter, so the token is taken before
  any replay read. But `backend.md`'s "a replay is never refused" is about in-progress caps and daily budgets, and
  those genuinely sit after the replay read — the facade's replay check runs before admission. Spending a rate
  token on a replay is ordinary rate limiting, not a rule violation.
- *A settled-short hold keeps its platform charge.* Deliberate, and asserted by
  `PlatformImageSpendAdmissionTests.A_settled_hold_is_not_given_back`.

**Open, for the owner:**
- *Whether a swept hold that had already reached a provider should keep its platform charge.* Fix 2 gives it back,
  which is right for every hold that made nothing and is the same reasoning the by-hand release was written for —
  but it reads lower than the "high, never low" principle where a pass billed pictures and then died without
  settling. That case is the accepted gap below, where the creator is charged nothing either.
- *The two pre-existing items from DL.7 stand*: a hold that lapses before settlement charges nothing for pictures
  already billed, and a worker inside a provider call when the sweep takes its lease can have a picture made but
  never charged.
- *Lease checks are reads, not conditional writes.* `RecordBilledVariantAsync` and its siblings load the operation
  and check the lease in memory; `GeneratedImageOperation` carries no row version, and EF returns an
  already-tracked instance without refreshing it. So a stale worker can overwrite a newer claimant's count or
  status, and the window is the load-to-save round trip rather than only time spent at the provider. This is the
  accepted item above, but wider than it was described.
